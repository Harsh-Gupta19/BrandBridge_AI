How this plan works

Three days a week is a real constraint, not a soft one. It means roughly sixteen focused hours per person per weekend, and it means anything left half-finished on Sunday sits untouched for four days and costs an hour of re-reading to resume. The plan is built around that.

The weekend shape

Friday evening (~3 h) — pull main, 20-minute stand-up, pick up the smallest independent task. No new architecture on a Friday.
Saturday (~7 h) — the deep work block. This is where the weekend's real output happens.
Sunday (~6 h) — finish, integrate, review each other's PRs, run the gate, write the log entry.

Two hard rules

Nothing is carried across a week boundary. If a task cannot finish by Sunday evening, split it on Friday so that some reviewable part can. Half-done work that waits four days is the main way three-day weeks fail.
Sunday 18:00 is merge-freeze. Everything reviewable merges by then. The last hour is the gate, the demo check and the contribution log.

The gate

Each weekend ends with a check that is demonstrable, not a feeling. Run it together in fifteen minutes. If it fails, the next weekend opens by fixing it — the plan bends, the gate does not. A failed gate carried silently into week 5 is how a capstone runs out of runway.

The contribution log

Each person adds three lines to docs/contributions/<name>.md every Sunday: what I built, what I decided, what I learned. Fifteen minutes a week, and it becomes the individual viva prep and the report's methods section. Reconstructing it in December from git history is far more work and far less convincing.

On the non-engineering half of the work

Dataset labelling, campaign and persona design, creator outreach for the consented gold set, test scenarios, evaluation rubrics, the experiment log and the final report are substantive capstone contributions, not overhead. They are scheduled into the lanes below as named tasks with hours, because work that is not on the plan does not happen on a three-day week.

The seven weekends at a glance

The mid-semester review lands inside weekend 2

Why the freeze is a week early on purpose

A capstone is not marked on how much was built. It is marked on whether the claim is defensible, the evidence is real, and each person can explain their own decisions. Four quiet weeks of writing and rehearsal are worth more than one more feature — and a team that is still merging code in the last week will present a system nobody has time to understand.

Weekly rituals

Friday, 20 minutes

Everyone states one sentence: what I will have finished by Sunday evening. Not what I will work on. If a lane cannot name a finishable thing, split it there and then — that is the single highest-value twenty minutes of the week.

Saturday, 10 minutes at midday

Blockers only. No status. If someone is stuck on another lane's interface, the fix is a mock or a stub agreed in that ten minutes, never a wait until Sunday.

Sunday 18:00, merge freeze

Everything reviewable is merged. Reviews happen throughout Sunday, not in a queue at the end. A PR older than one weekend is a process failure.

Sunday 19:00, the gate

Run the gate check together, on one machine, out loud. Then each person writes three lines in their contribution log. Then stop — four days off is part of the plan, and it is what makes the next Friday productive.
